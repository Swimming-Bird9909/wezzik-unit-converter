Metric vs Imperial: a 5-minute cheat sheet

Most of the world uses the metric system (grams, liters, kilometers, °C). The US still leans on imperial (ounces, cups, miles, °F). When a recipe, manual or parcel label mixes the two, these are the numbers worth memorizing:

  • Weight: 1 kg ≈ 2.205 lb · kg to lbs
  • Length: 1 inch = 2.54 cm · inches to cm
  • Temperature: 0 °C = 32 °F, 100 °C = 212 °F · °C to °F
  • Volume: 1 US cup ≈ 237 mL · cups to mL
  • Speed: 1 mph ≈ 1.609 km/h · mph to km/h

For anything precise, use the dedicated converters — rounding a cup to "240 mL" is fine for water, but not for flour.

5 kitchen conversions every home baker needs

Baking is chemistry, so volume-only measuring is the enemy of consistency. The conversions home bakers ask for most:

  1. Flour: grams to cups — one cup of all-purpose flour is about 120 g.
  2. Sugar: grams to cups — one cup of white sugar is about 200 g.
  3. Butter: grams to tablespoons — 14 g per tablespoon.
  4. Cups to mL — a US cup is 236.6 mL.
  5. Tablespoons to cups — 16 tbsp in a cup.

Tip: a $10 kitchen scale removes guesswork entirely. Weigh in grams, then convert only when a recipe demands cups.

Shipping & parcels: why unit mix-ups cost money

Cross-border sellers quote dimensions and weights in different units. A "2 lb" limit is 0.907 kg; a "30 cm" side is 11.81 inches. Getting these wrong triggers oversize fees. Keep these handy:

  • kg to lbs and lbs to kg
  • cm to inches and inches to cm
  • kg to stones (useful for UK parcel limits)

When in doubt, convert both ways and pick the conservative number for declared weight.
