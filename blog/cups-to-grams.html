Cups to Grams: The Ultimate Ingredient Conversion Chart

A cup of flour does not weigh the same as a cup of sugar, honey, or butter. Get the exact gram value for 50+ common baking ingredients, plus the one trick that makes every recipe turn out right.

Why "1 cup = X grams" has no single answer

A cup is a unit of volume. A gram is a unit of weight. They measure different things, so converting between them requires knowing the density of what you are measuring.

One US legal cup of water weighs 240 grams. One US cup of flour weighs about 120 grams. One US cup of honey weighs 340 grams. Same cup, three different weights — because flour, water, and honey pack differently.

This is why "convert 1 cup to grams" is the most-searched kitchen question on the internet, and why no single number can answer it.

The "spoon and level" method

Most US recipes assume flour is measured by the "spoon and level" method:

  1. Stir the flour in its bag or container to aerate it.
  2. Spoon it loosely into the measuring cup — do not pack.
  3. Level off the top with a straight edge (knife, spatula, chopstick).

If you scoop directly with the measuring cup, you can pack 20–30% more flour in. That is the difference between a soft cake and a tough brick.

International cup differences

The US "cup" is 236.588 mL. Many other countries use a 250 mL metric cup — about 5% larger. This is the most common silent cause of "why did my imported recipe fail?":

  • US legal cup: 236.588 mL (used in US recipes)
  • Metric cup: 250 mL (used in Australian, NZ, UK, Canadian recipes)
  • UK imperial cup: 284.131 mL (rarely used in modern UK recipes)
  • Japanese cup (ご飯一杯): 180 mL (rice-specific)

When following a US recipe, the values in the chart above apply. For a non-US recipe, scale by 1.057 (250/236.588) to convert between US and metric cups.

The $10 kitchen scale trick

Once you bake with a scale, you never go back. For under $10 you can buy a digital kitchen scale that reads in grams, and for under $20 one that does both grams and ounces.

European and Australian recipes are written in grams by default for exactly this reason. A 350 g loaf of bread always has the same ratio of flour to water, regardless of how you scoop. A "3 cups of flour" recipe depends entirely on whose cup, whose flour, and how aggressively you pack it.

Our recommendation: convert the recipe to grams once (using the chart above), then use the scale forever after.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams is 1 cup of flour?

120 g if measured spoon-and-leveled. 130–150 g if scooped directly. The difference is huge — always spoon and level.

How many grams is 1 cup of sugar?

200 g for granulated white sugar, 220 g for packed brown sugar, 120 g for powdered sugar.

How many grams is 1 cup of butter?

227 g, which equals two US sticks (each stick is 113 g or 8 tbsp).

Is a US cup the same as a UK cup?

No. US cup = 236.588 mL, UK cup = 284.131 mL (rare), metric cup = 250 mL. The 5–20% difference will noticeably change most recipes.

How do I convert grams to cups?

Divide the gram weight by the density of the ingredient. Use the chart above to find the per-cup weight, then divide your grams by that number. Or use our cooking converter for the reverse calculation.
