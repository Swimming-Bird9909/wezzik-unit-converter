Pounds to Kilograms: The Complete Conversion Guide

Whether you weigh yourself, ship a package, or follow a US recipe, you will meet the pound↔kilogram conversion more often than almost any other. Here is the exact factor, the formula, a quick-reference chart, and the mental math shortcut that works without a calculator.

The exact conversion factor

By international agreement since 1959, one avoirdupois pound is defined as exactly 0.45359237 kilograms. That is not an approximation — it is a fixed constant.

To convert pounds to kilograms, multiply by 0.45359237. To go the other way (kilograms to pounds), divide by 0.45359237, or multiply by 2.20462262.

The math is the same in both directions; only the operator flips.

The formula

There are two ways to write it:

  • kg = lb × 0.45359237 (pounds to kilograms)
  • lb = kg × 2.20462262 (kilograms to pounds)

For body weight, parcel weight, and most everyday uses, rounding to four decimal places is more than enough precision.

Mental math shortcut

When you do not have a calculator, two approximations are accurate to about 1% — close enough for nearly all real-world uses:

  • 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb (or 2.205 if you want one more digit)
  • 1 lb ≈ 0.45 kg (or 0.4536 for more precision)

So a 150-lb person weighs roughly 150 × 0.45 = 67.5 kg. The true value is 68.039 kg, so you are off by about 0.5 kg — well within "good enough" for everyday estimates.

For shipping, where small errors can mean oversize fees, use a real calculator (or our free kg to lbs converter) instead of the mental math.

Common mistakes to avoid

Mass vs. weight. In everyday English "weight" and "mass" mean the same thing, but in physics they are different. A pound is technically a unit of force (it depends on gravity), while a kilogram is a unit of mass. On Earth surface, the distinction is invisible, which is why every kitchen scale and bathroom scale uses the two interchangeably. Do not worry about it unless you are doing physics problems.

Pound-force vs. pound-mass. Engineers sometimes distinguish between lbf (pound-force) and lbm (pound-mass). Outside of engineering, every "pound" you encounter is lbm. The 1959 agreement we used above is the one for lbm.

Troy pounds and apothecaries' pounds. There are other historical "pounds" (troy pound = 373.24 g, used for precious metals; apothecaries' pound = 373.24 g). If you see "lb" in a gold or pharmacy context, double-check which one is meant. For cooking, shipping, body weight, and everything else, it is avoirdupois.

When precision matters

The 0.45359237 factor is exact, so any conversion done correctly is correct. The only errors come from rounding too early.

For most uses (body weight, recipes, casual shipping), rounding to the nearest 0.01 kg (10 g) is more than enough. For scientific or pharmaceutical work, use the full factor and round only at the very end.

Frequently asked questions

How many kg is 1 lb?

Exactly 0.45359237 kg, by international agreement since 1959.

How many pounds is 1 kg?

Exactly 2.20462262 pounds.

How do I convert kg to lb without a calculator?

Multiply by 2.2. For most everyday uses, that gives you a value within 1% of the exact answer.

Why is the factor 0.45359237?

It is the international avoirdupois pound, defined in the 1959 agreement between English-speaking countries to standardize trade. Before 1959, the UK pound and US pound were slightly different.

Is 1 lb the same on the moon?

A pound is a unit of mass (lbm), so the same pound of matter weighs less on the moon (less gravity). But the conversion to kg is unchanged — a pound of mass is still 0.45359237 kg anywhere.
